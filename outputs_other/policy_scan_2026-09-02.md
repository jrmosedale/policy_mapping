## Headline

**Four candidate records found, three of them recommended for addition. None is a post-window publication — all three predate the current workbook versions.**

That is the substantive finding of this scan, and it is more useful than a list of new items would have been: the gap is not that the workbooks have fallen behind since July, it is that **devolved primary legislation enacted in spring 2026 was never captured**. Two nations passed framework environment Acts — Scotland in March, Wales in April — and neither appears in the UK governance workbook, which as a result understates the UK's statutory nature-governance landscape at the point where it is changing fastest.

This validates the §7.4 decision to keep devolved administrations in the standing watchlist rather than scanning them on trigger only. Every one of this scan's findings came from that part of the watchlist. Had it been dropped, the scan would have returned nothing.

**Recommendation: treat the first pass as a backlog clearance, not a routine scan.** Quarterly cadence from here.

## Candidates

### 1. Natural Environment (Scotland) Act 2026 — **add** · `LEG`

- **Source:** [legislation.gov.uk 2026 asp 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2026/6/contents) · [Explanatory Notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2026/6/notes)
- **Royal Assent:** 12 March 2026 (passed 29 January 2026).
- **Why it matters here:** Part 1 requires Scottish Ministers to set **statutory nature-restoration targets** covering habitat condition, species status and environmental conditions, **and to establish accompanying monitoring indicators**. Reporting at least every three years; ten-yearly review of target suitability; independent scientific advice required for target-setting; Environmental Standards Scotland provides independent quality assurance. Parts 2–4 modify National Parks (adding cultural development to park aims; a duty on public bodies to *facilitate* National Park Plans rather than merely have regard to them; fixed-penalty byelaw powers), reform deer management (intervention grounds extended to nature-restoration projects; a mandatory national deer management and venison plan within five years), and cover swift nest boxes, marine protection, gulls and forestry.
- **Climate input:** indirect today; **likely yes in future**. The Act mandates indicators that do not yet exist. Habitat-condition and species-status measures of this kind routinely take climate inputs. This is a standing watch item — when Scottish Ministers publish the target set and its indicators, that is an `IFW` and a set of `IND` records, and it is exactly the project's core question.
- **Overlap:** joins the existing Scottish legislation cluster [LEG-032] Nature Conservation (Scotland) Act 2004, [LEG-033] Wildlife and Natural Environment (Scotland) Act 2011, [LEG-034] Environment (Scotland) Act 2023. Links to [ORG-014] NatureScot and [ORG-034] Scottish Government.
- **Confidence:** high on existence, assent date and provisions (primary source). Medium on the eventual indicator implications — the targets are not yet set.

### 2. Environment (Principles, Governance and Biodiversity Targets) (Wales) Act 2026 — **add** · `LEG`

- **Source:** [gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/environment-principles-governance-and-biodiversity-targets-wales-act-2026) · [legislation.gov.uk 2026 asc 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/4/enacted)
- **Royal Assent:** 27 April 2026.
- **Why it matters here:** three parts. Part 1 sets environmental principles and objectives that Welsh Ministers, Natural Resources Wales and other public authorities must apply. Part 2 creates **the Office of Environmental Governance Wales**, a new body responsible for public-authority compliance with environmental law. Part 3 amends the Environment (Wales) Act 2016 to establish a framework for **statutory biodiversity targets** aimed at halting and reversing biodiversity decline.
- **Climate input:** as for Scotland — indirect now, target-dependent later.
- **Overlap:** none in the workbook. Wales is currently thinly represented in the Legislation sheet relative to Scotland, which this partly explains.
- **Confidence:** high (primary source, both government and legislation.gov.uk).

### 3. Office of Environmental Governance Wales — **add** · `ORG`

- **Source:** created by candidate 2, Part 2.
- **Why it matters here:** the Welsh counterpart to [ORG-007] Office for Environmental Protection (England/NI) and to Environmental Standards Scotland. Without it the workbook's set of post-Brexit environmental governance bodies is incomplete, and the governance diagram shows an oversight gap in Wales that does not exist in reality.
- **Climate input:** n/a (governance body).
- **Overlap:** peer of [ORG-007]; sibling relationship to candidate 4.
- **Confidence:** high on creation. **Needs check** on operational status, leadership and commencement date before writing — the Act creates it, but a body created in statute may not yet be operating.

### 4. Environmental Standards Scotland (ESS) — **add** · `ORG`

- **Source:** [environmentalstandards.scot](https://environmentalstandards.scot/about-us/what-we-do/) · established under the UK Withdrawal from the European Union (Continuity) (Scotland) Act 2021, Part 2.
- **Why it matters here:** **this is a pre-existing gap, not a new development** — ESS has operated since 2021 and is named in candidate 1 as the independent quality assurer of Scotland's new nature-restoration targets. It is absent from Public Bodies. The workbook therefore holds England/NI's environmental watchdog ([ORG-007] OEP) but neither devolved equivalent.
- **Climate input:** n/a (governance body).
- **Overlap:** peer of [ORG-007] and of candidate 3. Links to [ORG-034] Scottish Government and to candidate 1.
- **Confidence:** high. Flagged as the clearest evidence that the devolved watchlist was earning its keep before this scan existed.

## Correction — 30by30 was missed, and why

Raised by Jonathan after the scan was filed. **Both documents are in scope and one is squarely inside the window.** This section records the failure rather than quietly fixing it, because the causes are methodological and would otherwise recur.

**What was missed**

| Document | Date | In window? |
|---|---|---|
| [30by30 on land: Delivery plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/30by30-on-land-in-england-delivery-plan/30by30-on-land-delivery-plan) | 13 July 2026 | **Yes** |
| [30by30 on land: assessing whether land can contribute](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/30by30-on-land-assessing-whether-land-can-contribute/30by30-on-land-assessing-whether-land-can-contribute) | 13 July 2026 | **Yes** |
| Criteria for 30by30 on land in England | 29 Oct 2024 (COP16) | No — pre-existing gap. Superseded the [Dec 2023 paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/delivering-30by30-on-land-in-england), withdrawn 29 Oct 2024 |

**Relevance — high, and directly on the project's core question.** The delivery plan sets a baseline (~7% of England already meeting the criteria, a further 3.5% expected soon, 9% at Silver tier), names Defra, Natural England, Protected Landscape Authorities and National Estate for Nature members as delivery leads, and commits to an assurance process and a 30by30 portal. The criteria require **monitoring of "condition and trends of important habitats and species"**, and SSSIs count only if in **favourable or recovering condition**. Habitat condition is climate-sensitive, so this is not a governance-only record: it is a policy whose qualifying test depends on measurements a climate service can inform. It is also England's delivery of **GBF Target 3**, measured internationally by `[GBF-013] 3.1 Coverage of protected areas and other effective area-based conservation measures` — already in the indicators workbook.

**Cause 1 — the scan was prior-driven, not enumerative.** I searched for publications I already expected: EIP, CCC, JNCC, OEP, new Acts, devolved legislation, IPBES, MCCIP. I never enumerated what Defra actually published between 6 July and 2 September. A keyword-led scan returns only what the scanner already thought of — the exact inverse of a scan's purpose. Critically, **this passed every documented step of the protocol**: old §6 step 4 said "work the watchlist, restricted to items published inside the window" without saying *how*, and §2 named organisations rather than enumerable date-filtered endpoints. A protocol that can be followed correctly and still miss everything unanticipated is a defective protocol, not just a bad run. **Fixed:** new §2.0 requires enumeration first and gives the gov.uk date-filtered query; §6 step 4 now requires per-source enumeration counts before any searching.

**Cause 2 — the overlap check accepts adjacent records as coverage.** `[POL-022] 30×30 Technical Working Group` exists, contains the string "30by30", and its `Link` field points at the 30by30 policy page. Any substring search for "30by30" therefore reports the topic as covered — but POL-022 is a `Coordination body / Working group`, and the commitment, its criteria and its delivery plan hold no record at all. The retrospective assessment sweep run the same day used exactly this method and would have made the same mistake. **Fixed:** §4 now requires an overlap match to be the same *kind* of thing, confirmed against `Record_Type` and `General_Type`.

**Cause 3 — I over-claimed on a null result.** This note's original headline asserted "None is a post-window publication". That conclusion was drawn from searches that never enumerated the window, so it was not supported by the method used. **Fixed:** §4 now requires null results to be reported with the method that produced them — "enumerated Defra publications 6 Jul – 2 Sep, 34 items, none in scope" is falsifiable; "no new Defra publications" is not.

**Status of the corrected enumeration — now run. See the second pass below.**

## Second pass — enumeration and ministerial statements

Run after the correction above, with the gov.uk endpoint approved and Jonathan's suggestion to scan government statements. **Eight further items queued, five of them records absent from the workbooks.** The second pass found more than the first.

### The gov.uk search API does not work from this environment

Worth recording precisely, because it changes what "enumerate" can mean here. `https://www.gov.uk/api/search.json` with `filter_organisations` and `filter_public_timestamp` returned **708,971 results** — the entire gov.uk corpus — with top hits including "Tax your vehicle" and "Check the MOT history of a vehicle". A control query (`?count=3&q=30by30`) returned the identical unfiltered set. **The fetch tool discards the query string**, so every filtered query silently degrades to an unfiltered one. The shell route is also closed: `curl` to gov.uk returns HTTP 403 from the egress proxy.

This is a nastier failure than a blocked endpoint, because it *returns a plausible-looking response*. An unwary scan would have recorded "enumerated Defra publications, nothing in scope" on the basis of a result set about vehicle tax. **Fixed in §2.0:** an implausible total or off-topic results now count as enumeration failure, not as a null result.

**What works instead:** the Atom feed at `gov.uk/government/organisations/<slug>.atom` — a plain path, no query string. It is a rolling recent window rather than a date range, so it reaches back only as far as it reaches; for this run, 19–20 August 2026 across 20 entries.

### Two structural findings that matter more than the individual records

**1. JNCC is effectively invisible to this method.** The gov.uk API returns **one** JNCC item for the whole window, and three for Forest Research. Both publish on their own domains (jncc.gov.uk, forestresearch.gov.uk), not gov.uk. ## Blocks 3–4 and CCC/OEP

### Block 3 — UKHSA, DESNZ, MHCLG, DfT, HM Treasury (908 items)

Record-bearing types screened.

**Corrected framing (2 September, after challenge).** This block was first written up as showing "a systematic asymmetry: the indicators workbook holds the *measure* while the governance workbook lacks the *instrument*". That claim was too strong and the vocabulary was invented rather than drawn from the schema — the governance workbook's `General_Type` already distinguishes Strategy/Framework, Scheme/Programme, Plan, Economic/market instrument and others, and "instrument" means something narrower there than the way it was used.

The four cases are **not equivalent**, and only one is a demonstrable gap:

| Case | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **UK Emissions Trading Scheme** | **Demonstrable gap** | `[ORG-013]` UK ETS Authority exists *to administer the scheme*, and `[LEG-014]` Energy Act 2023 and `[LEG-030]` GHG Emissions Trading Scheme Order underpin it. The workbook already asserts a chain whose middle link has no record — an internal inconsistency, not a judgement call. |
| **National Policy Statements** | **Scope question** | None held as a class; only `[LEG-054]` Planning Act 2008. Whether NPSs belong is a decision, not a defect. |
| **Greenhouse gas removals** | **Scope question** | `[IND-C-050]` exists because the CCC's framework tracks GGR. That is a fact about the CCC's framework, not evidence the GGR programme belongs in the governance workbook. |
| **Sustainable aviation fuel** | **Scope question** | Same reasoning as GGR for `[IND-C-041]`. The SAF Act 2026 stands on the legislative-backlog finding instead. |

**The general lesson, now written into §4 of the protocol:** an indicator's existence does not imply a missing governance record. The inference only holds where a held record's own fields *name* something that has no record — the orphan-reference problem, in prose rather than in `[CODE]` form. Otherwise the asymmetry is a scope question and should be queued as one, at medium confidence.

Confidence on the GGR, SAF mechanism and SAF Act rows has been corrected from High to Medium in the queue, with a correction row recording why.

Energy-system items (clean flexibility roadmap, corporate PPAs, ecodesign, EV excise duty, net-zero ports) are queued as low priority with a scope question attached: how far energy-system policy belongs in a climate–*nature* catalogue. Recorded so it is not reopened each quarter.

## Devolved administrations — method failure, and a workaround that found more than enumeration would have

**gov.scot's publication search ignores URL filters.** A request filtered to Environment for the window returned **51,044 results** — the entire corpus, newest first, across all topics. Same failure as the gov.uk department filter: the parameters are not the site's own. Under §2.0 that is an **enumeration failure, not a null**, and it is recorded as such.

**The workaround was better than the thing it replaced.** `gov.scot/policies/biodiversity/` is a pointer document in exactly the sense §2.0b describes for ministerial statements: it names a nation's strategies, plans, programmes and duties in one place. Three findings from one page:

- **Scottish Biodiversity Strategy to 2045** and **Scottish Biodiversity Delivery Plan 2024–2030** — neither held. England's equivalents (`[POL-001]` EIP, `[POL-003]` NBSAP) *are* held, so **the workbook has England's strategic layer and not Scotland's**. That is a coverage asymmetry a UK-scope catalogue should not have, and it compounds the two devolved Acts found in the first pass.
- **Scottish Biodiversity Programme** (Scottish Government with NatureScot) — no record.
- **Scotland's 30 by 30 commitment** — distinct from England's, delivered through NatureScot, no record. Taken with the queued England 30by30 delivery plan, this confirms 30by30 needs **per-nation records rather than one**.

**Protocol updated**: policy pages are now named alongside ministerial statements as pointer documents, and gov.scot's filter behaviour is recorded so the next scanner does not read 51,044 results as a successful query.
